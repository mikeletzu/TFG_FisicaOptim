\chapter*{Introduction}
\label{cap:introduction}
In recent years, the upsizing and complexity of videogames has involved the essential optimization and automation of many aspects of its development, both in the render and internal systems logic. Particularly, the evolution of 3D environments, under the premise of obtaining more detailed graphics, has implied a significant increase on computational cost. One way of reducing this cost to make simulations feasible is using ML models, which help estimating the behaviour of pertaining elements.

Pioneering harware and software companies have developed tecnologies to improve graphic performance by incorporating this type of algorithms. Among them are: NVDIA with DLSS (Deep Learning Super Sampling), which has been used in numerous videogames such as Resident Evil Requiem, which uses DLSS4 \footnote{ \textbf{\href{https://www.nvidia.com/es-la/geforce/news/dlss-4-rtx-path-tracing-game-announcements-ces-2026/}{Deep Learning Super Sampling:}}increases game performace, alowing it to render at a lower resolution, as this tecnhology will rebuild a higher resolution image in runtime. It uses tools such as  \textit{Ray Reconstruction} to replace its manual \textit{denoisers} or DLAA (Deep Learning Anti-Aliasing) to softer the edges.}; and AMD with FSR4 (FidelityFX Super Resolution)\footnote{ \textbf{\href{https://www.amd.com/en/products/graphics/technologies/fidelityfx/super-resolution.html}{FidelityFX Super Resolution Upscaling:}} open access tecnhique developed by AMD which boosts performance by image upscaling. It differs from NVDIA DLSS by having less specific hardware requirements, as well as a softer visual effect.}. Likewise, many companies take this into gameplay, simulating behaviours and mechanics, as one can see in EA (FIFA \footnote{\textbf{\href{https://news.ea.com/press-releases/press-releases-details/2021/EA-SPORTS-Introduces-FIFA-22-With-Next-Gen-HyperMotion-Technology-Bringing-Footballs-Most-Realistic-and-Immersive-Gameplay-Experience-to-Life/default.aspx}{HyperMotion}:} firstly introduced in FIFA22, it's a tecnhology that combines motion capture and machine learning in order to generate new animations in real time that help create a passionate, organic and fluid movement in game.}), Embark studios (Arc Raiders \footnote{\textbf{\href{https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/learning-to-move-physics-based-enemy-locomotion-in-arc-raiders/917319}{Physics-Based Enemy Locomotion:}} creates enemy movement thanks to reinforced learning, combining physics, robotic knowledge and bringing to life enemies with decision making ability when it comes to locomotion.}) and Ubisoft with its multiple linees of AI research. In this context, a third area of application currently being researched is the appliance of ML models in physics simulations.

In the field of physics simuation, with the increased computating power of current systems, greater fidelity is sought in the recreation of interaction between different elements. Aspects such as hair, fluids or cloth, which were highly simplified just over a decade ago, now aspire to greater realism. The physics simulation of their behaviour and characteristics results in a significant computational load, which affects directly video game performace, damaging the frame rate. The industry finds itself in a state-of-the art boom in relation to this issue, investigating and striving to apply ML as a solution. This can be seen, for example, in the work of Ubisoft La Forge, Ubisoft's research and development group. Among their research are recent applications of ML in various simulation fields, such as the adaptation of character animations to physical interactions or the acceleration of deformable body simulations, which will be discussed more througly in the next capter.
\section*{Motivation}
In the same context where other tecnhologies already used in games like NVDIA DLSS, where they appluy neural network models  in order to reduce computational load in render, some studies start to apply similar techniques in the industry in physics simulation. It is born in a context where, in order to raise profits, games are no longer only distributed to devices that have specific hardware and dedicated graphic cards, but to other platforms, which have not the same capactity. The use of these optimization techniques can allow these simulations to be recreated with an acceptable degree of fidelity on lower-powered devices such as the Nintendo Switch, handheld PCs, or mobile devices. Likewise, an increasing number of devices, even low-performance ones (compared to desktop consoles or gaming PCs), include some type of neural network acceleration hardware, such as the Nintendo Switch 2, which has 48 Tensor Cores, or the Xbox One X with an AMD XDNA NPU. This opens up a range of possibilities for running these models more efficiently.

The main motivation for this study lies in achieving visually realistic simulations that focus on user perception. Therefore, fabric or cloth simulation was chosen. Cloth simulation presents itself as a case of complex physics, high dynamism, and application in both optimization within embedded games and tools for artists, offering a wide range of uses. It is beginning to be integrated into large companies and continues in the experimental phase. This use case was chosen because it represents a suitable balance between physical complexity and familiarity with the visual result, allowing for both technical and perceptual analysis.

\section*{Metodología}
In this research, accessible and free-to-use tools will be employed to facilitate the reproducibility of the results obtained. The main development environment will be Windows 11, as it is the operating system used by the research team and widespread among potential users. The cloth simulation, as well as the integration of the artificial intelligence model, will be carried out in Unity (version 6000.3.2f1, newest long-term support version at the starting date of this project). Regarding programming languages, C\# will be used for development within Unity, including simulation, generation and data collection, while Python with PyTorch will be used for training the machine learning models. The integration of the trained models into the runtime environment will be run using the Unity Sentis package. GitHub, based on the Git version control system, will be used for source code management and collaborative work. The GitHub Projects tool will also be used for planning, assigning, and tracking tasks. Additional tools include Google Drive for storing documentation, meeting notes, and occasional work materials. Lastly, Discord will serve for team coordination through online meetings and weekly in-person gatherings.
