\section*{Conclusions}
This research was aiming to build an infrastructure that would enable the use of ML models to replicate cloth movement in real time in a videogame engin with low-latency. The goal was to identify the limitations of bringing techniques that haven´t been implemented outside of academic contexts to indie development.\newline

Using simple geometries and a collider in Unity, dynamic cloth simulations were created. The relevant data of the simulation was collected on run-time and recorded on CSV format. There was a total of 6 properties for each vertex, making up to 13 individual features: position, SDF (distance to the nearest colliding surface), velocity, SDF gradients or directions to the collider, UV coordinates and the max distance movement freedom from the base pose. Based on these datasets a model was trained. This model was able to learn the behaviour of the cloth, meaning to take on the rest positions and deformations produced by the collider without collapsing. \newline

A training environment was created using Pytorch, enabling the loading and preparation of the datasets, creation of the necessary structures (Pytorch datasets and models), training and testing of these, generation of usefull metrics and finally the export of the models in ONNX format. Essentially the model got a vector of the features of each vertex as an input and delivered displacement deltas for each one of them. Based on this foundation, the following techniques and models were developed to improve its performance:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Models:} A RNN and a MLP model were implemented. The \textit{many to one} architecture provided temporal perception to the models, enabling more coherent results.
    \item \textbf{Compounded error management:} The accumulated error was addressed through three different techniques applied in the training phase: injecting noise into the input thata, combining the unrolling technique with the back-propagation through time method and different loss functions.
    \item \textbf{Dimensionality reduction:} Principal component analysis was used to reduce the input size.
\end{itemize}

The Sentis library was used to load the final models into Unity. Unity´s Cloth component was succesfully replaced with the newly created ClothML component, moving the geometry according to the inferenced displacements by the model. Lastly an app was created as support for the expetiments, available on the project repository. \newline

With said pipeline, experiments on the most promising aspects were set up, aiming at the optimization and improvement of the visual result: the comparison on the RNN and MLP models, the consequence on the use of unrrolling, having the velocity as input and reducing intup with PCA.\newline

Visually, the outcome indicates that a combination of injected noise for the input, recurrence and unrrolling provide the simulation of stability and structural presevation over time. Applying PCA improves the presevation further more, it also optimizes the inference time. However, it does not reach the former´s realism. \newline

At last, a valid reaserch path is established, conforming to the evaluated context. User tests ensure that the behaviour of the outcome of the project is relatively close to that of a real fabric, without reaching its realism. The most usefull techniques for the reproduction of the simulation are distinguished, stablishing the following limitations and steps for future work.

\subsection*{Limitations}
The limitations of the research can be summed up in three main topics: the theoretical and practical approach to the simulation, the limited resources available given the scale of the project and the limitatios concerning the resulting models.\newline

The simulation chosen as a base resulted to be one of the most taxing parts of the project. As mentioned before, it is a highly dynamic simulation with a wide range of movements and inertia, causing various challenges. Some studies research these type of simulations in academia, specially the already mentioned \cite{bertiche2022neural}, however the reasearches focused on poses or tools fail to address them. Therefore, it was concluded that it could hardly be solved within the projects scope. The initial proposal did not account for this dimension. Additionally, this approach does not line up with the most common industry aplication, which there is not many public information about. Nowadays, simulations with excesive movements are yet handled with solvers, due to the instability and lack of detail the models generate. As a matter of fact, AI is used to reduce simulation costs for background characters or to create wrinkles as a base for 3D modelling artists. As mentioned in the \ref{sec:Rendimiento} section, the performance on inference time and the quality of the visual results are opposing, thus it was not possible to improve both concurrently. Besides the issue with inertia, it is important to mention that many techniques have been left out of the research such as physics-based loss functions or generalization in topology given their theoretical complexity or belonging to less generalist projects (a tool to create wrinkles over a basic cloth simulation, for example). \newline

On the other hand, the research bears the burdens of any small scale software development. There is no budget or training platforms. Even thoug it is part of the study´s approach, developing the entirety of the project on non-specialised laptops entailed various restrictions. The datasets were just enough to generate the appropriate inference for a small cloth simulation, still bigger datasets would allow for better results, provided the amount of training data is strongly related to the range of motion the model is familiar with. Given the limitations on time, budget and hardware, training datasets consisted of 25000 frames, while other studies benefit from larger databases reaching even $10^{6}$ frames \cite{holden_subspace_2019}. \newline

Apropos the former subject, the limitations of the developed models, the bottom-line is the impossibility of these to generalise topology nor a wide range of movements. The model is certainly able to face the simulation of a cloth it has been trained with, but it can not function propperly on a different mesh. Likewise, it is sensible to new events like prominent changes on the colliders behaviour. Additionally, the simulation of garments was outside of the scope of the projects, partly because of the difficulty of generating viable datasets with the chosen platform (Unity).
